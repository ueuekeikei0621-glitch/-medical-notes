## 1. ERC 2025 TCA algorithm, step by step, and how standard ALS is modified

### Takeaway
ERC 2025 Fig. 8 covers both traumatic cardiac arrest and peri-arrest. It is explicitly **not a sequential algorithm**:
- Once a non-traumatic cause is judged unlikely, CPR starts while every reversible cause (hypovolaemia, hypoxia, tension pneumothorax, tamponade) is treated **simultaneously**.
- Treating those reversible causes has **priority over chest compressions**.
- Resuscitative thoracotomy is gated by the "4E" rule: Expertise, Equipment, Environment, and Elapsed time < 15 min.
- Adrenaline is reduced to a possible "temporary bridge".
- REBOA is not recommended outside clinical trials.

### Cited Findings

**What changed since 2021**
- ERC Table 1 "Summary of changes", row "Traumatic cardiac arrest": 2021 = "New algorithm established / Invasive procedures introduced". 2025 = "Algorithm clarified. Role of chest compressions specified. REBOA de-emphasised" (p.3) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Key messages (guidelines at a glance, p.11)**
- "Traumatic cardiac arrest is different from cardiac arrest due to medical causes; this is reflected in the treatment algorithm (Fig. 8)." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- "The response to traumatic cardiac arrest is time-critical and success depends on a well-established chain of survival, including focused pre-hospital and specialised trauma centre care." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- "Early and aggressive management of reversible causes (e.g. haemorrhage control, airway management, chest decompression) is essential for survival." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- "Ultrasound aids in identifying the cause of cardiac arrest and guides resuscitative interventions." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- The general special-circumstances principle says: "Where appropriate, prioritise treating reversible causes, even if chest compressions are briefly interrupted." It also says to always address "hypoxia, hypovolaemia, electrolyte disorders, hypothermia, cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax, thrombosis, and toxic agents" (p.5) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Fig. 8 "Traumatic cardiac arrest algorithm" (p.12), transcribed from the figure image** — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
1. Top box: "Traumatic cardiac arrest / peri-arrest".
2. Decision: "Non-traumatic arrest likely?" → **YES** → "ALS" (standard ALS algorithm).
3. **NO** → main box. A vertical arrow labelled "START CPR" runs down its whole length. Headline: "Address reversible causes simulataneously [sic] / Prioritise treatment of reversible causes over chest compressions!"
4. Bullets inside the main box, in figure order:
   - "Control external catastrophic haemorrhage"
   - "Secure airway and maximise oxygenation"
   - "Bilateral chest decompression (thoracostomies)"
   - **Highlighted band:** "Relieve tamponade (penetrating chest injury)" and "Proximal vascular control (manual aortic compression)"
   - "Pelvic splint"
   - "Blood products / massive haemorrhage protocol"
5. A side box feeding into the main box lists the targets: "Hypovolaemia / Hypoxia / Tension pneumothorax / Tamponade".
6. An arrow from the highlighted band leads to a box reading "Expertise ? / Equipment ? / Environment ? / Elapsed time ? < 15 min", then to "Resuscitative thoracotomy", then to "ROSC ?".
7. Warning box: "Perform **interventions based on clinical priority,** not sequential".
8. "ROSC ?" → **YES** → "Pre-hospital: Immediate transport to appropriate hospital" / "In-hospital: Damage control surgery / resuscitation". **NO** → "Consider termination of resuscitation".

**Fig. 8 caption**
- The algorithm "should be used to guide the management of traumatic cardiac arrest (TCA) and peri-arrest trauma patients with impending circulatory collapse."
- "In contrast to the sequential ALS algorithms, it is a framework for context-sensitive decisions on clinically prioritised interventions."
- Examples given: "patients with a cardiac tamponade need urgent thoracotomy; patients with pelvic fracture in haemorrhagic shock but with a stable airway may benefit more from immediate application of a pelvic binder and rapid transfer without airway intervention" — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Chest compressions**
- "Immediate resuscitative efforts in traumatic cardiac arrest focus on simultaneous treatment of reversible causes, which takes priority over chest compressions." The evidence base is "nine systematic reviews and evidence updates and consensus from the expert group" (p.29) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- In hypovolaemia, tamponade or tension pneumothorax "the effectiveness of chest compressions is uncertain". In pseudo-PEA "chest compressions may even reduce the remaining cardiac output by further decreasing venous return… Therefore, chest compressions must not delay immediate treatment of reversible causes" (p.30) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Open cardiac compression:
  - After resuscitative thoracotomy, "open cardiac compressions might be beneficial".
  - Patients in TCA "within the first six hours of hospital admission may benefit from open cardiac compression instead of closed chest compressions".
  - This rests on one retrospective study showing higher long-term survival in both penetrating and blunt trauma, but "the study does not address the management of reversible causes" (pp.30–31) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Adrenaline (vasopressors and inotropes)**
- "the role of inotropes and vasopressors in traumatic cardiac arrest (TCA) is unclear. They may serve as a temporary bridge until reversible causes are addressed."
- Their use may also be considered in "late-stage shock with cardiac dysfunction or mixed shock states such as trauma with a neurogenic component".
- Treating reversible causes "should take preference over inotrope/vasopressor therapy" (p.31) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Who should run the TCA algorithm**
- "Providers without the training to manage reversible causes should follow the standard ALS algorithm and prioritise rapid transport under ALS or BLS, depending on system capability. Those with the necessary expertise should make case-by-case decisions…" (p.31) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Haemorrhage control, pelvic splint, blood products, aortic occlusion, REBOA (p.31)**
- "The main principle is to achieve immediate haemostasis." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Compressible external bleeding: "elevation (of a bleeding limb), direct or indirect pressure, pressure dressings, tourniquets and topical haemostatic agents". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Non-compressible bleeding: "splints (pelvic splint), blood products, IV fluids and tranexamic acid can be used while transferring the patient for surgical haemorrhage control." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Aortic occlusion: "Immediate aortic occlusion is recommended as a last resort measure in patients with exsanguinating and uncontrollable infradiaphragmatic torso haemorrhage." It can be done by resuscitative thoracotomy with cross-clamping (or manual compression) of the descending aorta, or by REBOA. "There is no evidence for one technique being superior to the other." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- REBOA: "A recent UK REBOA study in traumatic shock revealed a possible increase in mortality. The ERC does not recommend the use of REBOA outside of clinical trials." The study cited is Jansen et al., UK-REBOA RCT, JAMA 2023. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Blood: "In hypovolaemic traumatic cardiac arrest, immediate restoration of the circulating blood volume with blood products is mandatory." Prehospital plasma plus packed red cells "may improve survival, especially in cases with prolonged prehospital time". Recent evidence "suggests additional benefit from whole blood transfusion". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Airway, oxygenation and ventilation (pp.31–32)**
- Causes of hypoxaemia: "airway obstruction, traumatic asphyxia or impact brain apnoea". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Impact brain apnoea: UK NTARN data show "15 % survival rate with 90 % favourable outcome" in TCA with impact brain apnoea. A Dutch national study attributed 52 % of TCA to TBI-related apnoea. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Risks of controlled ventilation:
  - It raises intrathoracic pressure, which impedes venous return (especially in hypovolaemia) and reduces diastolic filling (tamponade).
  - It can convert a simple pneumothorax into a tension pneumothorax.
  - It increases venous bleeding.
  - — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Ventilation strategy: "Minimising intrathoracic pressure using low tidal volumes and minimal PEEP may help optimise cardiac preload." Ventilation should be monitored with capnography and adjusted to normocapnia. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- CAB over ABC: in a prospective observational study of exsanguinating injury with SBP < 90 mmHg, prioritising circulation over tracheal intubation was associated with lower 30-day mortality (17.5 % vs 72.0 %, p < 0.001) (p.31). — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Tension pneumothorax and thoracostomy (p.32)**
- "Tension pneumothorax is a reversible cause of cardiac arrest and must be excluded in traumatic cardiac arrest." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- It occurs in about 0.5 % of prehospital major trauma patients, and 13 % of those develop TCA. Diagnosis must be "based on clinical examination or POCUS". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- When it is suspected in cardiac arrest or severe hypotension, "chest decompression by open thoracostomy should be carried out immediately". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Technique: "perform bilateral thoracostomies in the 4th intercostal space at the midaxillary line, allowing extension to a clamshell thoracotomy if required." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Fallback: "Needle thoracocentesis should be attempted if the provider is not competent to perform a thoracostomy, or if the necessary surgical instruments are not immediately available." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Tamponade: pericardiocentesis vs thoracotomy (p.32)**
- Tamponade is "a frequent cause of cardiac arrest in penetrating chest trauma". Those with the ability, competence and equipment "can restore circulation with immediate resuscitative thoracotomy via a clamshell or left anterolateral incision". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Needle pericardiocentesis: a systematic review "concluded that there remains a limited role for this method in non-trauma centres where definitive surgical management is not immediately available." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Resuscitative thoracotomy is "not exclusively indicated" for tamponade. It is "also applicable for direct haemorrhage control, aortic cross-clamping, and open cardiac compressions." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Resuscitative thoracotomy: evidence, timing and the 4E rule (p.32)**
- Liu 2022 meta-analysis (49 studies):
  - Prehospital resuscitative thoracotomy more than 5 min after arrival on scene was associated with more neurological complications.
  - More than 10 min from initial encounter to thoracotomy was associated with higher mortality.
  - ISS ≥ 25 and absence of signs of life predicted worse outcomes.
  - Mortality was 93.5 % prehospital vs 81.8 % in the ED (P = 0.02).
  - In the ED, mortality was 92.8 % for blunt vs 78.7 % for penetrating trauma (P < 0.001).
  - The ERC concludes that "timely intervention within 5–10 min is crucial for improving outcomes."
  - — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- The "four E rule", verbatim — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753):
  - "Expertise: teams that perform resuscitative thoracotomy must be led by a highly trained and competent healthcare practitioner. These teams must operate under a robust governance framework."
  - "Equipment: adequate equipment to carry out resuscitative thoracotomy and to deal with the intrathoracic findings is mandatory."
  - "Environment: ideally resuscitative thoracotomy should be carried out in an operating room. Resuscitative thoracotomy should not be carried out if there is inadequate physical access to the patient, or if the receiving hospital is not easy to reach."
  - "Elapsed time: the time from loss of vital signs to commencing a resuscitative thoracotomy should not be longer than 15 min"
- "If any of the four criteria is not met, resuscitative thoracotomy is futile and exposes the team to unnecessary risks." — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Prehospital thoracotomy cohort:
  - 3.8 % survived with favourable neurological outcome, all of them with tamponade after penetrating trauma.
  - "There were no survivors beyond 15 min of traumatic cardiac arrest for cardiac tamponade and 5 min after exsanguination-induced cardiac arrest."
  - — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**System and in-hospital organisation (p.31)**
- Prehospital:
  - "stay and treat" vs "scoop and run" or "treat-as-you-go" "depends less on geography and more on the available skill set, equipment, and system infrastructure".
  - "prolonged prehospital time must be avoided". Early pre-alert of the Major Trauma Centre, and declare "Code Red" (or equivalent) to activate the massive transfusion protocol and the trauma team.
  - The ERC refers to the 2024 Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh (Faculty of Pre-Hospital Care) position statement for prehospital management (Weegenaar, Perkins, Lockey, SJTREM 2024;32:139).
  - — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- In-hospital: "all life-saving interventions are carried out in parallel rather than sequentially", by a multidisciplinary team (trauma surgeons, emergency physicians, anaesthetists, radiologists, intensivists, nursing). — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Preventable deaths: potentially preventable trauma deaths are 15.1 % (Germany), 29 % (US) and 20 % (Australia), with exsanguination the leading preventable cause. The ERC therefore recommends accredited trauma management training (pp.29–30). — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**Post-ROSC care (pp.32–33)**
- Early whole-body CT. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Damage control resuscitation: permissive hypotension, haemostatic resuscitation and damage control surgery. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Permissive hypotension: maintain BP "no more than is necessary to sustain a radial pulse" until surgical haemostasis. Use caution in TBI. "The duration of hypotensive resuscitation should not exceed 60 min". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Active warming against the "lethal triad". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Coagulation: early plasma, platelets, fibrinogen or whole blood, TXA, and point-of-care coagulation monitoring. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

**ERC Executive Summary**
- It reproduces the same algorithm as Fig. 17, with the same caption and the same key messages — [ERC 2025 Executive Summary](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110770)

**Paediatric differences (brief, from the ERC Executive Summary)**
- PBLS:
  - "Follow standard CPR, start opening the airway and ventilate."
  - Use a jaw thrust.
  - Stop external bleeding with pressure, haemostatic dressing or tourniquet.
  - "Use an AED only if there is a high likelihood of a shockable rhythm (e.g. following electrocution)."
  - — [ERC 2025 Executive Summary](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110770)
- PALS:
  - Use the acronym "HOTT" (Hypotension, Oxygenation (hypoxia), Tension pneumothorax and cardiac Tamponade). "In cardiac arrest, treating these has priority, or should run concurrently with, chest compressions and the administration of adrenaline IV/IO."
  - "Perform bilateral thoracostomies in traumatic cardiac arrest with or without signs of a tension pneumothorax." The technique is finger thoracostomy in the 4th or 5th intercostal space, anterior axillary line.
  - — [ERC 2025 Executive Summary](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110770)

### Inferences
- How standard ALS is modified, in practice:
  - Compressions: they continue (CPR is "started"), but they are subordinate. Interrupting or deferring them to perform a thoracostomy, thoracotomy or haemorrhage control is endorsed.
  - Adrenaline: it is not part of the TCA algorithm box at all. At most it is a bridge.
  - Defibrillation: not specifically modified. It is implicit in "ALS" for suspected non-traumatic or shockable arrest (VF is about 4 %).
  - Airway: it is one parallel task among several, not automatically "A first". A CAB approach and minimal intrathoracic pressure are favoured.
- Wording difference between adult and paediatric guidance: the adult text says reversible-cause treatment "takes priority over" compressions and puts vasopressors behind it. The paediatric text says "priority, or should run concurrently with" compressions *and* adrenaline. The adult guidance is the more aggressive of the two in de-emphasising compressions and adrenaline.
- The 15-minute figure appears three times in the ERC text: the 4E "Elapsed time" limit (loss of vital signs to start of thoracotomy), the withholding criterion (no signs of life within the preceding 15 min), and the prehospital thoracotomy outcome data (no tamponade survivors beyond 15 min). The 5-minute figure (no survivors after exsanguination-induced arrest) argues that thoracotomy for haemorrhagic arrest has a much narrower window than for tamponade.

### Gaps
- The ERC TCA section has **no explicit statement on defibrillation**, such as shock timing or whether to prioritise rhythm checks. I found only epidemiology (VF 4 %) and shockable rhythm listed as a favourable prognostic factor.
- The ERC gives **no explicit adrenaline dose or timing** for TCA, and no statement on whether to omit it entirely.
- ECPR in TCA is not addressed in the ERC TCA section I read. A 2025 SJTREM meta-analysis on ECPR in TCA exists ([Europe PMC record, PMC12849657](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13049-025-01534-9)), but I did not review it.
- I did not read the RCSEd FPHC 2024 prehospital TCA position statement that the ERC cites.

## 2. Resuscitation Council UK 2025 TCA algorithm: exact wording and differences from ERC

### Takeaway
The RCUK 2025 "Adult traumatic cardiac arrest/peri-arrest" algorithm is essentially the ERC Fig. 8 with UK formatting. The RCUK Special Circumstances page cites the ERC 2025 Special Circumstances paper as its reference. The main differences are wording:
- The entry question is "Traumatic cardiac arrest likely?" rather than "Non-traumatic arrest likely?".
- A "Minimise time on scene" prompt is added.
- The reversible causes are set out as **H**ypovolaemia / **O**xygenation / **T**amponade/**T**ension pneumothorax (HOTT, with bold initials).
- The word "simultaneously" is dropped from the headline.

### Cited Findings

**RCUK algorithm, box by box (checked against the rendered PDF image)** — [RCUK TCA algorithm PDF](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Traumatic%20cardiac%20arrest%20algorithm%202025.pdf)
1. Title: "Adult traumatic cardiac arrest/peri-arrest" (Guidelines 2025).
2. Decision: "Traumatic cardiac arrest likely?" → **NO** → "Continue with ADULT ADVANCED LIFE SUPPORT (ALS)". A separate dotted box beside it reads "Minimise time on scene".
3. **YES** → main box with a "Start CPR" arrow down the side. Headline: "Address reversible causes / Prioritise treatment of reversible causes over chest compressions".
4. Bullets in the main box:
   - "Control external catastrophic haemorrhage"
   - "Secure airway and maximise oxygenation"
   - "Bilateral chest decompression (thoracostomies)"
   - **Bold sub-box:** "Relieve tamponade (penetrating chest injury)" and "Proximal vascular control (manual aortic compression)"
   - "Pelvic splint"
   - "Blood products/massive haemorrhage protocol"
5. Side box of targets: "**H**ypovolaemia / **O**xygenation / **T**amponade/ **T**ension pneumothorax".
6. From the bold sub-box → "Expertise? Equipment? Environment? Elapsed time? < 15 min" → "Resuscitative thoracotomy" → ROSC decision.
7. Warning box: "Perform interventions based on clinical priority, not sequential".
8. "Return of spontaneous circulation? (ROSC)" → **YES** → "Pre-hospital: Immediate transport to appropriate hospital" / "In-hospital: Damage control surgery/ resuscitation". **NO** → "Consider termination of resuscitation".

**RCUK Special Circumstances page (published 27 Oct 2025)**
- It states that traumatic arrest is managed differently from medical arrest and that the algorithm "can also be used in peri-arrest situations". — [RCUK Special Circumstances 2025](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)
- It says "The response to traumatic cardiac arrest is time-critical", and that early, aggressive management of reversible causes (haemorrhage control, airway management, chest decompression) is essential. — [RCUK Special Circumstances 2025](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)
- It says ultrasound helps identify the cause and guide interventions. — [RCUK Special Circumstances 2025](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)
- The fetch tool's summary said clinicians should focus on correcting reversible causes "but continue chest compressions unless they delay or prevent that correction". This is the tool's paraphrase, not verified verbatim wording. — [RCUK Special Circumstances 2025](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)
- The page's reference is "European Resuscitation Council Guidelines 2025, Special Circumstances in Resuscitation (Resuscitation 2025;215 (Suppl 1):110753)". — [RCUK Special Circumstances 2025](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)
- The page text, as fetched, did not include detail on thoracotomy criteria, REBOA, adrenaline, termination criteria and similar topics. These are carried in the algorithm, or by reference to the ERC paper. — [RCUK Special Circumstances 2025](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/special-circumstances-guidelines)

**ERC Fig. 8, for comparison**
- Entry question "Non-traumatic arrest likely?" (YES → ALS).
- Headline "Address reversible causes simulataneously [sic] / Prioritise treatment of reversible causes over chest compressions!"
- Side box "Hypovolaemia / Hypoxia / Tension pneumothorax / Tamponade".
- No "minimise time on scene" box. The ERC text instead says "prolonged prehospital time must be avoided".
- — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

### Inferences
- Clinically the two algorithms are the same: the same seven interventions, the same 4E gate with < 15 min, the same ROSC branches and the same "consider termination" endpoint.
- The default question is framed differently. ERC asks whether a *non-traumatic* cause is likely, which makes trauma the default once there is a trauma context. RCUK asks whether *traumatic* arrest is likely.
- RCUK adds an explicit scene-time prompt.
- RCUK's HOTT layout matches the ERC paediatric mnemonic. ERC adult uses "Hypoxia" where RCUK uses "Oxygenation".
- RCUK defers detailed criteria (4E definitions, 20-minute termination, REBOA position) to the ERC paper, since it cites that paper as its reference.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm whether the RCUK web page has a separate "what's different from ERC" statement. None was found in the fetched text.
- I could not verbatim-verify the RCUK page sentence on continuing compressions.
- I did not check the RCUK Adult ALS 2025 page for trauma-specific text.

## 3. AHA 2025 (and ILCOR CoSTR): what is said about cardiac arrest due to trauma

### Takeaway
The AHA 2025 Part 10 abstract lists every special circumstance the chapter covers, and **trauma is not among them**. In 2025, the AHA does not appear to provide a dedicated adult TCA algorithm or recommendations. I could not access the full text to confirm that no trauma recommendation appears elsewhere. I found no ILCOR CoSTR on TCA among the evidence the ERC cites.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Part 10, "Adult and Pediatric Special Circumstances of Resuscitation", Circulation 2025;152(Suppl 2):S578–S672, published 2025-10-22. Authors Cao D, Arens AM, … Lavonas EJ, … McBride ME. — [PubMed 41122889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41122889/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001380)
- The abstract's full scope list is: "anaphylaxis, asthma, cardiac arrest in the cardiac intervention suite, cardiac arrest following cardiac surgery, drowning, electrocution, gas embolism, high-consequence respiratory pathogens, hyperkalemia, hyperthermia, hypothermia, left ventricular assist device failure, pregnancy, pulmonary embolism, and poisoning…" plus alternatives to CPR and ECMO for poisoned patients. **Trauma is not listed.** — [PubMed 41122889](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41122889/)
- The AHA 2025 guideline hub lists Part 10 among the chapters. — [AHA 2025 guidelines hub](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2025-aha-guidelines-for-cpr-and-ecc)
- One trauma-related BLS airway statement appears in a secondary blog summary, which attributes it to Adult BLS airway management: "If jaw-thrust fails in trauma, head-tilt–chin-lift is acceptable despite cervical-spine concerns." This is a secondary source that I have not verified against AHA text. — [EM Ottawa blog summary](https://emottawablog.com/?p=14033)
- General (not trauma-specific) AHA 2025 adrenaline messages reported by secondary sources: epinephrine stays a strong recommendation with no change in dose or timing. For shockable rhythms it is reasonable to give it after initial defibrillation attempts fail. — [EMS1 summary](https://ems1.com/ems-training-and-education/mechanical-cpr-stay-and-play-epi-and-more-top-takeaways-from-the-2025-aha-cpr-ecc-guidelines)
- ILCOR: the ERC says its TCA evidence is "nine systematic reviews and evidence updates and consensus from the expert group". The references cited (250–258) are independent journal systematic reviews:
  - Lalande 2021, POCUS
  - Liu 2022, resuscitative thoracotomy
  - Tran 2020, prognostic factors
  - Vianen 2022, prehospital TCA
  - Wongtanasarasin, epinephrine
  - Morgan 2024, whole blood
  - and others
  - Its withholding and termination criteria are explicitly "based on expert consensus". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Outside scope, but it exists: ANZCOR has a dedicated guideline "11.10.1 Management of Cardiac Arrest due to Trauma". I did not review its content. — [ANZCOR 11.10.1](https://www.anzcor.org/home/adult-advanced-life-support/guideline-11-10-1-management-of-cardiac-arrest-due-to-trauma)

### Inferences
- For a US-oriented resident, practical TCA guidance comes from the trauma and EMS societies (ACS-COT, NAEMSP, ACEP; see section 5), not from AHA ACLS.
- The ERC/RCUK algorithm is the only one of the major resuscitation-council algorithms reviewed here that is specific to TCA.

### Gaps
- I could not read the AHA Part 10 full text (HTTP 403 from ahajournals.org, professional.heart.org and cpr.heart.org). I therefore cannot exclude a brief trauma mention, for example in Part 9 Adult ALS, Part 7 BLS (c-spine and airway), or the systems-of-care and termination sections.
- I found no ILCOR 2025 CoSTR specific to TCA. A targeted search of ilcor.org or the CoSTR 2025 tables was not done within the budget.

## 4. Peri-arrest and hypovolaemic shock steps, and recognising TCA

### Takeaway
ERC and RCUK both apply the same algorithm to TCA **and to peri-arrest trauma patients with impending circulatory collapse**. There is no separate peri-arrest algorithm.

Recognition rests on three things:
1. A plausible traumatic mechanism or signs of injury. Otherwise use standard ALS.
2. Ultrasound, to separate true arrest from low-output "pseudo-PEA" and to find a reversible cause.
3. Knowing the deterioration sequence: bradycardia and hypotension → pseudo-PEA → PEA without cardiac motion → asystole.

### Cited Findings
- "For a diagnosis of TCA, there must be plausible signs of traumatic injury or, at least, a mechanism of injury consistent with trauma. In the absence of an identifiable mechanism or visible signs of injury, the standard ALS algorithm should be followed instead." Ultrasound aids "in differentiating peri-arrest from TCA, identifying reversible causes, and guiding resuscitative efforts" (p.29). — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- The algorithm "applies not only to patients in traumatic cardiac arrest but also to peri-arrest trauma patients who present with signs of impending circulatory collapse" (p.31). — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- The RCUK algorithm is titled "Adult traumatic cardiac arrest/peri-arrest". — [RCUK TCA algorithm PDF](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Traumatic%20cardiac%20arrest%20algorithm%202025.pdf)
- Progression to irreversible circulatory failure (p.30) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753):
  1. Progressive bradycardia and hypotension (from severe hypoxia and acidosis).
  2. "PEA with minimal cardiac activity on ultrasound (Pseudo-PEA)": "the heart exhibits weak contractile activity on ultrasound but fails to generate an effective pulse or circulation".
  3. PEA with no cardiac activity on ultrasound: "The mortality of this condition approaches 100 %".
  4. Asystole.
- Causes and rhythms (p.30) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753):
  - Causes: uncontrolled haemorrhage 48 %, tension pneumothorax 13 %, asphyxia 13 %, tamponade 10 %.
  - Presenting rhythms: PEA 66 %, asystole 30 %, VF 4 %.
- ERC Table 8 pathophysiology: hypovolaemic shock (inadequate preload), tension pneumothorax (impaired venous return), tamponade (restricted ventricular filling), hypoxia or asphyxia (bradycardia → PEA → asystole). Contributing factors are metabolic acidosis and neurogenic shock. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Peri-arrest hypovolaemic shock management:
  - The caption example: pelvic fracture in haemorrhagic shock with a stable airway → pelvic binder and rapid transfer *without* airway intervention.
  - CAB-first evidence: SBP < 90 mmHg, 30-day mortality 17.5 % vs 72.0 %.
  - Damage control resuscitation and permissive hypotension (radial pulse; ≤ 60 min; caution in TBI).
  - — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- In-hospital the ERC lists vasopressors as possibly useful in late-stage shock with cardiac dysfunction or mixed or neurogenic shock, but secondary to treating the cause. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- US 2025 joint statement: when the cause is unclear, "particularly without clear signs of life-threatening trauma, standard basic and advanced cardiac life support (BCLS/ACLS) treatments for medical cardiac arrest is appropriate." Monitor and POCUS placement "should occur after indicated LSIs have been appropriately performed". — [NAEMSP/ACS-COT/ACEP 2025, PubMed 40068140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40068140/)

### Inferences
- In practice, a pseudo-PEA patient, or a peri-arrest patient with a palpable or Doppler-detectable output, should be managed as TCA. That means treating reversible causes in parallel. Given the ERC warning that compressions may reduce residual output, aggressive closed compressions should be avoided while cardiac motion is present.
- The US statement puts POCUS *after* life-saving interventions. The ERC treats ultrasound as an aid that guides interventions. Both agree that ultrasound must not delay haemorrhage control or decompression.

### Gaps
- Neither ERC nor RCUK defines numeric peri-arrest triggers in the TCA section, such as an SBP threshold or ETCO2. Such a threshold appears only in the ERC operating-room section (SBP < 50 mmHg with falling ETCO2), which is a different context.

## 5. Termination and withholding of resuscitation in TCA: ERC/RCUK criteria and the US joint statements

### Takeaway
The criteria differ by guideline:
- **ERC 2025 (expert consensus)**: consider **withholding** if there have been no signs of life for 15 min or the injuries are incompatible with survival. **Terminating** is suggested if there is no ROSC, or no ultrasonographic cardiac activity in PEA, **20 min after reversible causes have been addressed**.
- **RCUK**: the algorithm says only "Consider termination of resuscitation" if there is no ROSC.
- **US (current)**: the 2025 NAEMSP/ACS-COT/ACEP joint statement replaced the 2012/2013 statements. It rejects any universal time cut-off and says neither rhythm nor mechanism alone should be used to stop.

### Cited Findings
**ERC 2025 (p.29)** — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Consider withholding resuscitation in TCA if any of these apply:
  - "no signs of life within the preceding 15 min."
  - "massive trauma incompatible with survival (e.g. decapitation, extensive cardiac destruction, massive head injury with loss of brain tissue)."
- "We suggest termination of resuscitative efforts if there is:"
  - "no ROSC within 20 min after reversible causes have been addressed."
  - "no detectable ultrasonographic cardiac activity in PEA 20 min after reversible causes have been addressed."
- The ERC also notes that ACS and NAEMSP recommend withholding in trauma patients "presenting with apnoea, pulselessness and without organised ECG activity". It adds: "However, neurologically intact survivors initially presenting in this state have been reported." The source cited is the Millin et al. 2013 resource document ([J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2013](https://doi.org/10.1097/TA.0b013e31829cfaea)).

**RCUK 2025**
- At the NO branch after the ROSC decision, the algorithm says "Consider termination of resuscitation". No numeric criteria are shown on the algorithm. — [RCUK TCA algorithm PDF](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Traumatic%20cardiac%20arrest%20algorithm%202025.pdf)

**US: 2012 NAEMSP/ACS-COT "Termination of Resuscitation for Adult Traumatic Cardiopulmonary Arrest"** — [NAEMSP/ACS-COT 2012, DOI](https://doi.org/10.3109/10903127.2012.695857); [NAEMSP PDF](https://naemsp.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Case-21_NAEMSP-PS-TOR-for-Traumatic-Arrest.pdf)
- Publication: Prehosp Emerg Care 2012;16:571. Approved by ACS-COT on 9 Mar 2012 and by the NAEMSP Board on 16 May 2012.
- "Resuscitative efforts should not prolong on-scene time."
- "Termination of resuscitation may be considered when there are no signs of life and there is no return of spontaneous circulation despite appropriate field EMS treatment that includes minimally interrupted cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR)."
- "Past guidance has indicated that up to 15 minutes of CPR should be provided before resuscitative efforts are terminated, but the science in this regard remains unclear."
- "Implementation of termination-of-resuscitation protocols mandates active physician oversight."
- "Termination of resuscitation may be impractical after transport has been initiated."

**US (latest): NAEMSP/ACS-COT/ACEP joint position statement and resource document, "Prehospital Management of Adults with Traumatic Out-of-Hospital Circulatory Arrest"** — [PubMed 40068140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40068140/); [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10903127.2024.2428668); [Annals of Emergency Medicine version, PubMed 39984237](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39984237/)
- Publication: Breyre AM, et al. Prehosp Emerg Care, published online 2025-03-11. A joint position statement version also appears in Ann Emerg Med 2025.
- It was "written as an update to the 2013 joint position statements".
- Life-saving interventions (LSIs) to prioritise:
  - "External hemorrhage control with direct pressure, wound packing, and tourniquets"
  - Airway management "using the least-invasive approach necessary"
  - "Chest decompression if there is clinical concern for a tension pneumothorax. Empiric bilateral decompression, however, is not indicated in the absence of suspected chest trauma."
  - "External chest compressions may be considered but only secondary to other LSIs."
  - "Epinephrine should not be routinely used, and if used should not be administered before other LSIs."
  - "If point-of-care ultrasound (POCUS) demonstrates no evidence of cardiac motion, this may have utility in TOHCA management for prognostication."
- Withhold resuscitation for:
  - "Injuries that are incompatible with life (e.g., decapitation, hemi-corpectomy, incineration, open skull injury with extruding brain matter)."
  - "Evidence of prolonged circulatory arrest (e.g., rigor mortis, dependent lividity, decomposition)."
  - DNR/DNAR/Allow Natural Death orders.
- Discontinuation:
  - "Mechanism of injury should not be used as the sole determinant to discontinue resuscitation efforts."
  - "Electrical rhythm should not be used as the sole determinant… Of note, non-shockable rhythms (Pulseless Electrical Activity/Asystole) are associated with an extremely low likelihood of return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) or survival with neurologic recovery."
  - "There is insufficient evidence to support any specific universal standardized time-based cutoffs to discontinue resuscitation efforts based on the duration of resuscitation or transport times to definitive care."
  - Decisions are to be "locally determined based on EMS and trauma system resources and proximity".
- "Individual patient cost and organ donation should not be a factor in EMS clinical decision-making on-scene."
- Related 2025 NAEMSP compendium paper: "Addressing the Use of Mechanical Compression Devices in Traumatic Out-of-Hospital Circulatory Arrest" (Jasti J, et al., Prehosp Emerg Care, published online 2025-05-12). I could not retrieve its abstract. — [PubMed 40323299](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40323299/)
- Paediatric: a separate ACEP/ACS/NAEMSP/AAP statement covers withholding or terminating resuscitation in paediatric out-of-hospital traumatic arrest, originally approved in September 2013. — [ACEP policy statement](https://acep.org/patient-care/policy-statements/withholding-or-termination-of-resuscitation-in-pediatric-out-of-hospital-traumatic-cardiopulmonary-arrest2)

### Inferences
- How the ERC and US 2025 statements differ:
  - Time cut-off: the ERC gives a concrete 20-minute clock that starts *after reversible causes are addressed*. The US statement explicitly rejects universal time cut-offs.
  - Rhythm: the US statement warns against using rhythm alone, though asystole and PEA carry an extremely low likelihood of benefit. The ERC also tempers the older ACS/NAEMSP rule (apnoeic, pulseless, no organised ECG → withhold) by noting that intact survivors have been reported.
  - Chest decompression: the US statement says empiric bilateral decompression is not indicated without suspected chest trauma. ERC/RCUK list bilateral thoracostomies as a standard algorithm action, and ERC PLS applies them "with or without signs of a tension pneumothorax".
- Where they agree:
  - Compressions and epinephrine are secondary to LSIs.
  - Absent cardiac motion on POCUS is a prognostic marker. The ERC uses it as an explicit termination criterion at 20 min.
  - Do not prolong scene time.
- The older "up to 15 minutes of CPR" rule (NAEMSP/ACS-COT 2012) is superseded by the 2025 US statement's position of no universal cut-off.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full resource-document text of the 2025 US statement, only the complete structured abstract with its recommendations. Supporting statistics in the resource document were therefore not captured.
- I could not access the Annals of Emergency Medicine version's text, to check whether its wording differs.
- RCUK does not publish termination criteria separately from the ERC, as far as I found.

## 6. Prognostic factors cited by these guidelines

### Takeaway
ERC 2025 Table 7 lists negative and positive prognostic factors:
- **Negative**: age, female sex, higher ISS, head injury, shock on admission, transfusion need, CPR in the ED, children.
- **Positive**: emergency surgery, major trauma centre care, shockable or organised rhythm, reactive pupils, respiratory activity, spontaneous movements, shorter CPR, shorter prehospital time, penetrating chest injury, witnessed arrest, cardiac motion on ultrasound.

The strongest single bedside discriminator these guidelines use is **absence of cardiac motion on ultrasound**. Mortality then "approaches 100 %"; the ERC uses it as a termination criterion and the US statement as a prognostic marker. ETCO2 is not cited as a prognostic tool in the TCA sections reviewed.

### Cited Findings
- ERC Table 7 "Prognostication in traumatic cardiac arrest" (p.30; refs 254, 262–270). Column headers "−" and "+" were verified on the rendered page. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
  - **− (unfavourable):** Age; Female; Increased ISS; Head injury; Shock on admission; Need for blood transfusion; CPR in the emergency department; Children.
  - **+ (favourable):** Emergency surgery; Major trauma centre care; Shockable ECG rhythm; Reactive pupils; Respiratory activity; Spontaneous eye or extremity movements; Organised ECG rhythm; Shorter CPR duration; Reduced prehospital time; Penetrating chest injury; Witnessed arrest; Cardiac motion in ultrasound.
- Outcomes overall (p.29) — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753):
  - TCA is 4 % of prehospital cardiac arrests in Europe. Registry survival ranges from 0 % to 37 %.
  - A systematic review of 36 studies and 51,722 patients found overall mortality of 96.2 %, with 43.5 % of survivors neurologically favourable.
  - Physician presence on scene was associated with 6.1 % vs 2.4 % survival, and 57.0 % vs 38.0 % favourable neurological outcome.
  - "good neurological outcome is reported in less than 50 % of those who survive".
- Ultrasound: PEA without cardiac activity on ultrasound has mortality that "approaches 100 %". Pseudo-PEA (weak contractility without a pulse) is a distinct, potentially reversible state. The ERC cites the Lalande 2021 SHoC systematic review on POCUS as a predictor of outcome in TCA. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Resuscitative thoracotomy prognosis: penetrating trauma does better than blunt (ED mortality 78.7 % vs 92.8 %). ISS ≥ 25 and no signs of life are worse. Delays over 5–10 min are worse. Prehospital survivors were exclusively penetrating-tamponade cases. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Impact brain apnoea has a relatively good prognosis: 15 % survival, 90 % of survivors favourable (NTARN). — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
- Transport and prehospital time:
  - "Reduced prehospital time" is a favourable factor, and "prolonged prehospital time must be avoided". — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)
  - RCUK: "Minimise time on scene". — [RCUK TCA algorithm PDF](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Traumatic%20cardiac%20arrest%20algorithm%202025.pdf)
  - US 2025: there is insufficient evidence for transport-time-based cut-offs. — [PubMed 40068140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40068140/)
- Rhythm: non-shockable rhythms (PEA/asystole) carry an "extremely low likelihood" of ROSC or neurologically intact survival, but should not be the sole reason to stop. — [PubMed 40068140](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40068140/)
- ETCO2 in the ERC TCA section appears only as capnography to guide ventilation to normocapnia, not as a prognostic threshold. — [ERC 2025 SC](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2025.110753)

### Inferences
- For a resident at the bedside, the factors that combine to favour aggressive resuscitation, including thoracotomy under the 4E conditions, are:
  - a witnessed arrest with a short time since loss of vital signs (< 15 min, ideally within 5–10 min)
  - a penetrating chest mechanism or tamponade
  - signs of life, or an organised or shockable rhythm
  - cardiac motion on ultrasound
- Blunt exsanguination arrest with no cardiac motion and a prolonged interval is the profile in which the ERC/US criteria support withholding or stopping.

### Gaps
- Table 7 lists factors without effect sizes. The underlying odds ratios (e.g., Tran 2020 meta-analysis in Resuscitation 2020;153:119–35) were not extracted.
- I found no guideline-endorsed ETCO2 threshold for TCA prognosis in ERC, RCUK or the US statements.
- The AHA 2025 full text was not accessible, so any AHA prognostic statements on trauma could not be checked.
