# Hybrid Emergency Room (ハイブリッドER / HERS) for severe trauma and traumatic cardiac arrest: workflow, decision points, outcomes, and adoption (Japan first, then international)

Research cut-off: November 2026. Publication years are given in brackets. Abbreviations: HERS = Hybrid Emergency Room System; IVR-CT = interventional radiology CT (sliding-gantry CT plus C-arm on one table); WBCT = whole-body CT; AO = aortic occlusion; RT = resuscitative thoracotomy; DCS = damage control surgery; TAE = transcatheter arterial embolization; TCA = traumatic cardiac arrest; JTDB = Japan Trauma Data Bank.

Method note: Most numbers come from PubMed abstracts and open-access full texts (PMC / Europe PMC) that I read directly. Items marked "(search summary only)" come from search-engine summaries I could not open and check in full.

---

## 1. Origin and design: Osaka General Medical Center (大阪急性期・総合医療センター) 2011, components, and how it changes the conventional JATEC flow

### Takeaway
Osaka General Medical Center installed the first Hybrid ER in August 2011. It puts a sliding-gantry CT and a C-arm over one carbon-fibre table inside the trauma resuscitation room, so resuscitation, WBCT, DCS, TAE and burr-hole surgery all happen on one table without moving the patient. Compared with the JATEC/ATLS flow, it drops the chest and pelvic X-rays, does "CT first" even in shock if systolic BP stays ≥70 mmHg, and goes straight to RT if it does not. Later designs (dual-room, rotating table) were built to fix room occupancy and lack of surgical working space.

### Cited Findings
**Origin and equipment**
- In August 2011 Osaka General Medical Center installed a multi-slice IVR-CT system (Aquilion CX, TSX-101A; Toshiba Medical Systems) in its trauma resuscitation room. It has a carbon-fibre fluoroscopic table, a self-propelled C-arm and a sliding-gantry CT. The authors named it the "Hybrid ER" because it combines "examination" and "treatment" in one space. [Kinoshita et al., Ann Surg 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6325752/)
- The JA-HERS founding-members review [2019] dates the first HERS installation to July/August 2011 at Osaka General Medical Center. It describes HERS as a system in which "all life-saving procedures… whole-body CT examination, damage control surgery, and transcatheter arterial embolization… on the same table in the same room without transferring the patient." [JA-HERS, Acute Med Surg 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)
- **Concept**: JA-HERS stresses that temporarily using an existing angio-CT suite or hybrid OR as a resuscitation space "is insufficient in fulfilling the original HERS concept." The core is "a special angio-CT suite designated exclusively for… possibly critical patients combined with a well-trained team." [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)
- **Room components** listed in Wada et al. [2023]: (A) sliding CT scanner, (B) CT examination and intervention table, (C) moveable C-arm, (D) 56-inch monitor, (E) ultrasound, (F) mechanical ventilator. Airway management, emergency surgery and TAE are all done on that table. [Wada et al., Acute Med Surg 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/)
- **Functional definition**: a Hybrid ER is "an emergency unit with four functions — performing resuscitation, CT, surgery, and angiography." [Watanabe et al., WJES 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34174929/); also an "integrated system including an ER, emergency CT and IVR rooms, and operating rooms." [Watanabe et al., SJTREM 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30223859/)
- **Japanese description**: HERS places an IVR-CT in the emergency initial-treatment room (救急初療室). From initial care through CT, TAE, thoracotomy/laparotomy, pelvic external fixation and burr-hole craniotomy, treatment happens "患者移動なしで" (without moving the patient). IVR-CT itself was developed in Japan in 1992 and was first used for TACE. [稲垣直之 (済生会横浜市東部病院 放射線部), INNERVISION 2022;37(12):50](https://www.innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2022/iv202212_050.pdf)

**How the flow changes versus conventional JATEC (Osaka protocol, as published)**
- **Conventional period (2007–2011)**: Osaka used JATEC, which is based on ATLS. After the primary survey the surgeon decided whether the patient could go to the CT on the same floor. Only haemodynamically stable patients or rapid responders went to CT. Unstable patients, or those with clear FAST or X-ray findings, had bleeding-control surgery in the resuscitation room and CT afterwards. CT including transfer took about 20 minutes. Craniotomy, thoracotomy and laparotomy were generally done in the OR, and TAE in the adjacent angiography room. [Kinoshita 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6325752/)
- **Hybrid ER period (Aug 2011–)**:
  - The key concept was "immediate diagnosis with a whole-body CT and prompt surgical management for both head and trunk injuries."
  - Airway and breathing were fixed at once while circulation was assessed. In shock, obstructive shock (tamponade or tension pneumothorax) was looked for and ruled out first.
  - Physicians then judged whether the patient "could tolerate a few minutes of CT scanning." The threshold was systolic BP 70 mmHg despite fluid resuscitation. If CT was acceptable, WBCT was done "even if the patient was in a shock status." If SBP >70 could not be kept, an emergency RT was done.
  - DCS, TAE and burr-hole craniostomy were done on the CT/intervention table. Craniotomy and definitive surgery stayed in the OR.
  - "No chest or pelvic radiographs were obtained during the initial trauma work-up."
  - [Kinoshita 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6325752/)
- **Japanese framing**: in conventional JATEC, primary survey comes first and CT follows, which takes about 20 minutes including transfer to the CT room. HERS shortens this substantially. [INNERVISION 2024 (奈良県立医科大学附属病院 高度救命救急センター)](https://innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2024/iv202412_052.pdf)
- **Authors' interpretation**: the benefit is attributed "not [to] the whole-body CT… but the novel trauma workflow — comprised of an immediate CT examination, damage control surgery, and IVR without any patient transfer." [Kinoshita 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6325752/)

**Design evolution**
- **Dual-room (second-generation) HERS**, first at Kansai Medical University General Medical Center in July 2017. Two rooms are separated by a moveable door and the sliding CT moves between them. While surgery or IVR is under way in the Hybrid ER, the CT can scan another patient next door. This addresses the problem that "one room is occupied by one severely injured patient… for long periods." [Wada et al., SJTREM 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29506552/); [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)
- **Table-rotated type** (Shimane University, August 2017):
  - The C-arm sits on the wall opposite the CT gantry to free up working space, and the table rotates when IVR is needed.
  - It uses an operating table rather than an IVR table.
  - The room is an air-conditioned OR (class 10,000), with a three-bed trauma bay next door for multiple victims.
  - [Watanabe et al., SJTREM 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30223859/)
- **Barriers to installation**: space (a machinery room and control room are needed in addition to the treatment space) and funding of roughly US$0.5–2 million. Most sites installed HERS during major hospital or ED renovations. Floor areas range from 72.9 to 123.6 m² in total, of which 45.5–110.4 m² is treatment space. [JA-HERS 2019, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)
- **JA-HERS (日本ハイブリッドER研究会 / Japanese Association for Hybrid Emergency Room System)** was launched on 21 June 2018 in Kyoto. Its aims are to standardize HERS-specific trauma protocols, educate trauma teams and eliminate preventable trauma deaths. [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)

### Inferences
- The decisive change from JATEC is the order and location of steps, more than the equipment. Primary-survey X-rays and FAST-driven triage to CT or the OR are replaced by "fix A/B, exclude obstructive shock, then CT if SBP ≥70." The ~20-minute CT trip disappears.
- "CT first" in the original Osaka protocol was explicitly conditional. Patients who could not keep SBP >70 went to RT before CT, so it was never "CT for everyone, including arrest."

### Gaps
- I could not access the full Supplemental Digital Content figure of the Osaka room layout.
- I found no published standard floor plan or minimum equipment list from JA-HERS beyond the 2019 review table.

## 2. Published workflow / protocols: step by step, who is in the room, CT during CPR or for unstable patients, and when REBOA, TAE, RT, laparotomy or DCS are done in the room

### Takeaway
Published workflows share four features:
- The patient is moved once, still on the backboard, onto the CT/catheter table.
- Lines, labs and A/B interventions happen at once.
- Immediate WBCT is done if the patient is judged CT-tolerant.
- Haemostasis (TAE, DCS, REBOA, RT, external fixation, burr hole) happens on the same table.

For patients at risk of imminent arrest, JA-HERS's 2021 guidance (as summarized by Wada 2023) says RT or REBOA should come before CT. No validated haemodynamic CT-tolerance criterion exists. CT under mechanical CPR is described in non-traumatic arrest (Hyogo ECPR protocol), but I found no published protocol for CT during CPR in TCA.

### Cited Findings
**Step-by-step arrival workflow (Japanese hospitals)**
- **済生会横浜市東部病院 (Inagaki, 2022)**:
  - On arrival the patient is moved, still on the backboard, onto the catheter table (カテーテル寝台), which serves as the treatment table.
  - Monitors are attached immediately, peripheral IV access is placed, and blood for labs is drawn from the femoral artery (大腿動脈からの血液検査用採血).
  - Life-saving procedures follow, then trauma WBCT. If haemostasis is needed, TAE, thoracotomy/laparotomy, pelvic external fixation and burr-hole craniotomy are done without moving the patient.
  - [INNERVISION 2022](https://www.innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2022/iv202212_050.pdf)
- **Kansai Medical University Medical Center (Maruyama 2024)**:
  - "The medical team leader is a trauma surgeon who performs both RT and REBOA."
  - On arrival: fluids, intubation and chest drainage as needed, then plain and contrast pan-scan. DCS, TAE, external fixation and burr-hole craniotomy are planned and done in the Hybrid ER.
  - "In patients with impending CPA, the lead physician determines whether to proceed with resuscitation including AO before performing CT based on the patient's response to fluid resuscitation."
  - [Maruyama et al., Acute Med Surg 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10825068/)
- **Osaka General Medical Center (2011–2015 protocol)**:
  - A/B first, rule out obstructive shock, then use SBP 70 mmHg despite fluids as the CT-tolerance threshold.
  - Below 70, do emergency RT. DCS, TAE and burr hole are done on the table, with no chest or pelvic X-ray.
  - [Kinoshita 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6325752/)
- **JA-HERS guidance 2021 (as summarized)**: "the first edition of guidelines on ECPR and initial trauma care in the hybrid ER were established in 2021." The trauma guidance stresses assessing and treating impending A/B/C problems before safely doing CT:
  - "In severe trauma patients at risk of imminent cardiopulmonary arrest, resuscitative thoracotomy (RT) or… REBOA should be preferred before CT scan."
  - "There is currently insufficient evidence about criteria for RT or REBOA in HERS."
  - "There is no report… about the criteria of tolerable circulatory dynamics to perform CT scans in the hybrid ER; each emergency physician should carefully judge."
  - [Wada et al., Acute Med Surg 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/)
- **Saiseikai Yokohama-shi Tobu "CT-first resuscitation" (CTFR) [2026]**: defined as "immediate WBCT in a CT-equipped trauma resuscitation room with prespecified triggers for hemorrhage control." The abstract does not list the triggers. [Matsumoto et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42385208/)

**Who is in the room (team roles)**
- **JA-HERS**: "trauma surgeons, emergency physicians, radiologists, anesthesiologists, nurses, radiological technologists, and other paramedical staff." HERS-specific team-based learning is "absolutely essential." [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)
- **Osaka**:
  - The trauma team is trauma surgeons, neurosurgeons, orthopaedic surgeons and intensivists.
  - "All trauma surgeons have been trained to perform not only general surgery but also IVR." IVR was done by trauma surgeons and neurosurgeons, except specialized procedures such as aortic stenting or coronary intervention.
  - The authors attribute short time-to-TAE partly to "we did not have to wait for a radiologist."
  - [Kinoshita 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6325752/)
- **Wada 2023**: beyond the usual surgery, EM and anaesthesia members plus orthopaedics and neurosurgery, the room "requires personnel who can perform emergency transarterial embolization, including nurses and radiology technicians." Sites should train general surgeons in emergency IR to cover 24/7 and should use multidisciplinary conferences and simulation, including an extended-reality simulation [Ito 2021]. [Wada 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/)
- **Teikyo University**: interventional radiologists were present at patient arrival in 79% of HERS cases versus 22% of non-HERS cases. The authors call for "new team building involving the addition of IRs to the traditional trauma resuscitation team." [Ito et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31804417/); see also simultaneous DCS and endovascular procedures as "new multidisciplinary trauma team building" [Ito et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30308539/)
- **Saiseikai Yokohama-shi Tobu**: the figure of their hybrid ER labels emergency physician, medical intern, paramedic, registered nurse and radiology technician around the table. For rAAA they describe a "hybrid multidisciplinary team" of vascular surgeons, anaesthesiologists and emergency physicians. [Murai et al., J Vasc Surg Cases Innov Tech 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7902278/)

**Decision point: CT first or aortic occlusion / surgery first (near-arrest patients)**
- **Kansai Medical University (Maruyama 2024)**:
  - Of 2,353 trauma patients (May 2016–Feb 2023), 73 had RT with cross-clamping or REBOA. After excluding prehospital CPA, prophylactic REBOA and similar cases, 13 were analysed: median ISS 45, median TRISS Ps 34%.
  - **CT-first group (n=6)**: arrival to AO 36 min, to CT 11 min. Ps 49%; actual 28-day survival 50%.
  - **AO-first group (n=7)**: arrival to AO 7 min, to CT 27 min. Ps 20%; actual 28-day survival 57%.
  - **Subgroups**: CT → AO → haemostasis 3/4 survived; CT → haemostasis → AO 0/2; AO → CT → haemostasis 1/4; AO → DCS → CT 3/3 (arrival to intervention 9 min).
  - CT scan time itself was median 5–6 minutes.
  - Conclusion: in low-Ps patients, survival "might be better if resuscitation including AO is performed before CT and if damage control surgery is performed first before CT."
  - [Maruyama 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10825068/)
- **AO devices**: Japanese HERS sites use both RT with cross-clamp and REBOA in the room. In Maruyama's cohort RT was used in 54% and REBOA in 62%, with some patients receiving both. [Maruyama 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10825068/)
- **Case, Tohoku-affiliated group [2026]**:
  - Blunt right-ventricular rupture with tamponade, treated with RT and pericardiotomy in a hybrid ER.
  - The patient arrested despite manual compression of the rupture and open cardiac massage. VA-ECMO was started during ongoing open cardiac massage 12 minutes after arrest, followed by primary repair.
  - Later imaging showed blunt aortic injury, an unstable pelvic fracture and thoracic spine fractures, treated in stages. The patient was discharged at 6 months, living independently.
  - [Tanikawa et al., Am J Emerg Med 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42731460/)

**CT during CPR (mostly non-traumatic data)**
- **Hyogo Emergency Medical Center ECPR protocol (from January 2020)** for PEA patients who are ECPR candidates:
  - Mechanical CPR runs continuously with an automatic chest compression device (CLOVER3000; KOHKEN Medical).
  - "Head and chest CT scans were immediately conducted without scout images under mechanical CPR." If CT showed no intracranial haemorrhage, pericardial effusion or massive pleural haemorrhage, VA-ECMO was started. Otherwise conventional CPR continued without ECMO.
  - Of 12 PEA patients, 9 were diagnosed on CT (ICH 3, tamponade from aortic dissection 3, aortic rupture 2, cardiac rupture 1) and unnecessary ECMO was avoided. Of the 3 who received ECPR, 2 survived with favourable neurology.
  - At this centre "all trauma patients are taken to the hybrid ER whenever it is available."
  - [Ijuin et al., SJTREM 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9158146/)
- **Timing**: in that strategy, median time from hospital arrival to the end of CT was 170 seconds. [Okada et al., Resusc Plus 2024 (review)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10801325/)
- **Conceptual ECPR workflow (Osaka)**: ACLS flows into ECPR and then CT, CAG and PCI without transfer. Fluoroscopy allows cannulation "during ongoing CPR" and may reduce vascular complications. [Hayashida et al., BMC Cardiovasc Disord 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6964082/)
- **Nara (2024)**: in cardiac-arrest patients, ECMO can be started after CT confirmation, so CPR and treatment run in parallel. Expected indications are severe trauma, internal haemorrhage and ECPR. [INNERVISION 2024](https://innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2024/iv202412_052.pdf)

### Inferences
- In near-arrest trauma the published Japanese approach is graded:
  - CT-tolerant (Osaka used SBP ≥70 despite fluids): CT first, then targeted haemostasis.
  - Not tolerant or arresting: RT or REBOA first, with DCS before CT where the source is obvious. The CT then happens on the same table once there is some perfusion.
  - The Hybrid ER removes the transport penalty of CT but not the time penalty of scanning (5–6 min) or the physiological penalty of delaying aortic control.
- The Hyogo "scout-less head-and-chest CT under mechanical CPR" protocol is the only detailed published CT-during-CPR workflow. It is for non-traumatic PEA. Extrapolating it to TCA is inference only: in hypovolaemic TCA, chest compressions and CT time may matter less than immediate haemorrhage control and AO.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain the JA-HERS 2021 guideline text (ECPR and initial trauma care in HERS) or any thresholds it gives. Only Wada 2023's summary is cited.
- I found no published Saiseikai Yokohama-shi Tobu protocol document. The "prespecified triggers" in CTFR are not given in the abstract.
- I found no published Hybrid ER protocol that states what to do on arrival in established TCA (no pulse on arrival), such as RT vs REBOA vs CT under mechanical CPR, or how long to continue.

## 3. Outcome evidence: Kinoshita (Ann Surg 2019), later studies, meta-analysis, and data on cardiac arrest or near-arrest patients

### Takeaway
All mortality evidence is retrospective, mostly single-centre and all from Japan:
- The Osaka studies show adjusted 28-day mortality OR about 0.5, fewer exsanguination deaths, and time to CT cut from about 26 to 11 minutes.
- A 2025 meta-analysis of 5 Japanese studies gives OR 0.57 for in-hospital mortality, with high heterogeneity and risk of bias.
- Several studies show faster haemostasis and less transfusion without a mortality difference (Shimane, Teikyo pelvic, Saiseikai CTFR).

Patients in established TCA on arrival were excluded from the main outcome studies. Near-arrest data come from a 13-patient series. Arrest-related outcome data come only from non-traumatic ECPR studies.

### Cited Findings
**Osaka General Medical Center (single centre, historical control)**
- **Kinoshita et al., Ann Surg [2019; epub 2017]**: 696 consecutive severe blunt trauma patients (ISS ≥16): Conventional (Aug 2007–Jul 2011) n=360 vs Hybrid ER (Aug 2011–Jul 2015) n=336.
  - 28-day mortality: adjusted OR 0.50 (95% CI 0.29–0.85; P=0.011).
  - Deaths from exsanguination: adjusted OR 0.17 (0.06–0.47).
  - Time to CT: 26 (21–32) vs 11 (8–16) min.
  - Time to emergency procedure: 68 (51–85) vs 47 (37–57) min.
  - "Cases of traumatic cardiopulmonary arrest on arrival" were excluded, as were penetrating trauma, children under 15 and pregnant women.
  - [Kinoshita 2019 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28953551/); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6325752/)
- **Learning curve**: in the restricted cubic spline, estimated mortality "rose temporarily during the first year after the HERS installation and then dropped below the baseline." [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)
- **Umemura et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg [2021]**: same centre, Aug 2007–Jan 2020, 1,050 patients (360 conventional vs 690 Hybrid ER).
  - 28-day mortality: Ps-adjusted OR 0.48 (0.32–0.71).
  - Mortality "sharply decreased approximately 200 days after installation."
  - The benefit grew with severity (interaction P=0.014): survival was higher in the Hybrid ER group once ISS exceeded 25.
  - [Umemura 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33165282/)
- **Severe TBI**:
  - Unfavourable 6-month GOS-E was reduced after HERS: aOR 0.42 (0.18–0.93) [Kinoshita, World Neurosurg 2018, as summarized in JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/).
  - Long-term follow-up [2026]: 127 patients; time to CT 11 vs 27 min, time to surgery 48 vs 69 min; favourable GOS-E adjusted OR 4.47 (1.47–13.60). [Mitsuyama et al., Injury 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42562704/)
- **Concurrent bleeding control and ICP monitoring**: 10 patients, 4 (40%) survived to discharge. (Search summary only) [Kinoshita et al., WJES 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6267909)
- **Cost-effectiveness** (patients without severe TBI): gain of 1.03 QALYs and US$33,591 extra lifetime cost, giving an ICER of $32,522/QALY. It is cost-effective if the 28-day mortality OR is below 0.66, with 79.3% probability in probabilistic sensitivity analysis. [Kinoshita et al., WJES 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33413503/)

**Other Japanese centres**
- **Kansai Medical University**: time to CT 10 vs 29 min, to DCS 45 vs 108 min, to TAE 54 vs 75 min (Hybrid ER vs conventional). [Wada et al., cited in Wada 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/)
- **Shimane University (2016–2019, ISS ≥16)**:
  - Time to CT 25 vs 6 min; to intervention 101 vs 41 min.
  - In-hospital survival 96.9% vs 96.3% (NS); no preventable trauma deaths in either group.
  - Transfusion lower with Hybrid ER, maintained after propensity matching (RBC 8 vs 2 units).
  - [Watanabe et al., WJES 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34174929/)
- **Teikyo University (pelvic angioembolization)**:
  - Arrival to AE 46 vs 103 min; mortality 13% vs 15% (NS).
  - HERS survivors had lower TRISS Ps (61% vs 93%), meaning more severely injured patients survived.
  - [Ito et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31804417/)
- **Saiseikai Yokohama-shi Tobu CTFR [2026]**: 2019–2023, 1:1 propensity match with JTDB (n=248 per group).
  - Time to CT 0.4 vs 29.0 min; to first haemostatic intervention 53.7 vs 134.0 min.
  - Adjusted 24-h RBC −0.84 units (95% CI −1.65 to −0.03). In the shock subgroup, −3.76 units (−6.44 to −1.09).
  - In-hospital mortality 9.7% vs 8.9% (NS).
  - [Matsumoto et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42385208/)

**Pooled evidence**
- **Systematic review and meta-analysis [online Dec 2025]**:
  - 5 studies, all retrospective and all from Japan.
  - In-hospital mortality OR 0.57 (0.41–0.78).
  - Time to CT SMD −1.99; time to first haemostasis SMD −1.01; RBC SMD −0.15.
  - "Substantial heterogeneity and risk of bias."
  - [Kim et al., Int J Surg 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41405346/)
- **Multicentre work**: A KAKEN-funded project [2020–2024] (PI Fujimi, Osaka General Medical Center, with Kansai Medical University, Osaka University, Tohoku University and Osaka Medical & Pharmaceutical University) built a shared multicentre database. Its reported publication output is only a 2023 conference presentation. [KAKEN 20K10422](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/ja/grant/KAKENHI-PROJECT-20K10422)
- **Contextual counter-evidence on early CT**: in the JTDB (n=13,802, ISS >15), CT within 0–9 minutes was not associated with lower mortality than later CT. This study excluded cardiac arrest on arrival, patients needing DCS and deaths within 24 h, so it does not test Hybrid ER workflows in unstable patients. [Nishimura et al., Eur J Trauma Emerg Surg 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40140101/)

**Cardiac-arrest and near-arrest specific data**
- **Trauma near-arrest (aortic occlusion needed)**: 13 patients; 28-day survival 50% (CT-first) vs 57% (AO-first). Prehospital or on-scene CPA was excluded. [Maruyama 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10825068/)
- **Non-traumatic OHCA and ECPR in a hybrid ED (Tohoku, 2013–2022)**:
  - Installation was associated with shorter arrival-to-ECPR time over time. There was an initial upward level change just after installation, a learning-curve effect.
  - No significant difference in 30-day favourable neurological outcome or cannulation complications.
  - [Nakata et al., Resusc Plus 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11408869/)
- **Time to ECPR**: over 40 min before Hybrid ER, within 25 min after (single before-after study). [Okada 2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10801325/)
- **PEA and ECPR with CT under mechanical CPR**: 12 patients; CT identified 9 irreversible causes before ECMO. [Ijuin 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9158146/)
- **REBOA versus RT in traumatic OHCA (not Hybrid ER specific; JTDB 2004–2019)**:
  - 1,483 t-OHCA patients with aortic occlusion: REBOA n=144, survival to discharge 3.5%; RT n=1,339, survival 0.7%.
  - IPW-adjusted OR 3.73 (1.90–7.32).
  - A Saiseikai Tobu trauma surgeon (Funabiki) was a co-author.
  - [Yamamoto et al., JACEP Open 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33000081/)
- **REBOA in impending TCA (ABOTrauma registry, 16 centres including Japanese ones)**: n=74; SBP rose to about 90 mmHg after inflation; 36.6% survived. [McGreevy et al., Shock 2020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31851119/)

### Inferences
- Mortality benefit is consistent at Osaka (OR about 0.5 in two analyses of overlapping cohorts) but not replicated with a significant mortality difference at Shimane, Teikyo or Saiseikai Tobu. The consistent finding across sites is shorter time to CT and haemostasis and less transfusion.
- The meta-analysis pools overlapping single-country retrospective cohorts, so its OR 0.57 should not be read as robust.
- For TCA specifically there is no comparative outcome evidence that a Hybrid ER improves survival. The flagship studies excluded TCA on arrival. The only near-arrest series (n=13) is too small to say whether CT first or AO first is better, though it is the basis of the "AO/DCS before CT when Ps is low" recommendation.

### Gaps
- I found no published study of TCA on arrival managed in a Hybrid ER (survival, ROSC, time to AO, CT findings) from Osaka, Kansai Medical University, Saiseikai Tobu or the JA-HERS group.
- I found no prospective or cluster-randomized Hybrid ER trial; Wada 2023 calls for one. No peer-reviewed results from the KAKEN multicentre database were found.
- I did not retrieve full texts of Kim 2025 (which 5 studies, exact I²) or Matsumoto 2026 (CTFR triggers, whether TCA patients were included or excluded).

## 4. Adoption in Japan, and 済生会横浜市東部病院 (Saiseikai Yokohama-shi Tobu Hospital)

### Takeaway
Japan went from 1 Hybrid ER (2011) to about 10–11 (2018–2019), 19 (May 2022), 21–24 (2023) and "about 30" (end of 2023 to 2024). Counts differ between sources and no official registry figure was found.

Saiseikai Yokohama-shi Tobu Hospital does have a Hybrid ER: a dual-room (2-ルーム型) Canon IVR-CT HERS installed in October 2017 as part of its Yokohama City Severe Trauma Center role. Its staff have published on its use (about 1,000 patients/year, about 40% cardiac arrest) and on a "CT-first resuscitation" strategy [2026]. I did not find a hospital-website page describing the HERS or any TCA-specific protocol.

### Cited Findings
**National counts (chronological; note the discrepancies)**
- 2019 review: "To date, 11 HERS have been installed (10 in Japan and 1 in Korea)." Table lists by year: Osaka General Medical Center (Aug 2011), Tokyo Metropolitan Bokutoh, Yonemori Hospital, Jichi Medical University Saitama Medical Center, Hyogo Emergency Medical Center, Teikyo University, Kansai Medical University General Medical Center (dual-room), Shimane University, **Saiseikai Yokohamashi Tobu Hospital (dual-room, Oct [2017])**, Tohoku University. [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)
- As of September 2019: 11 tertiary emergency hospitals in Japan and 1 trauma centre in Korea. [Hayashida 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6964082/)
- 2021: "Japan's experience in 15 centers." [Palacios-Rodríguez et al., Colomb Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8216051/)
- As of May 2022: 19 facilities in Japan (2022年5月現在，国内の19施設で稼働). [INNERVISION 2022](https://www.innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2022/iv202212_050.pdf)
- 2023 review: "21 trauma centers in Japan have now added a hybrid ER." [Wada 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/); contrast "As of March 2023, 24 tertiary care hospitals in Japan have a hybrid ER." [Maruyama 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10825068/)
- Late 2023 to 2024: "approximately 30 centers in Japan" [Okada 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10801325/); "日本国内では約30の医療施設にHERSが導入" [INNERVISION 2024](https://innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2024/iv202412_052.pdf)
- Recent examples: Nara Medical University introduced a Canon HERS in May 2024, with time from ER entry to CT 14 → 3 min and to haemostasis 47 → 25 min [INNERVISION 2024](https://innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2024/iv202412_052.pdf). Tokyo Medical and Dental University introduced a 2-room type in October 2023 (vendor case study, search summary only) [Siemens Healthineers](https://www.siemens-healthineers.com/jp/clinical-specialities/surgery/customer-voices/tmd-medhospital-case).

Tomohiro Funabiki (船曵知弘, Saiseikai Yokohamashi Tobu Hospital) is a JA-HERS founding member. [JA-HERS 2019, Table 1 and Appendix](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/)

The paper reports a rAAA case with door-to-intervention 35 min, including endovascular balloon occlusion, EVAR and decompressive laparotomy in the same room. [Murai, Matsumoto, Egawa, Funabiki, Shimogawara, J Vasc Surg Cases Innov Tech 2020 (published Dec 2020)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7902278/)

## 5. International adoption (RAPTOR, North American and European hybrid suites) and guidance on using hybrid rooms for TCA

### Takeaway
Outside Japan the closest equivalents are trauma hybrid operating rooms (Calgary's RAPTOR, 2013; UF Health; Toronto; Koblenz). Most are angio-capable ORs, often without an in-room sliding CT, and are entered after the resuscitation bay. Single-centre data show faster haemorrhage control and fewer transfusions without a mortality difference. Sliding-CT trauma rooms (Koblenz) shorten time to CT but did not change time to bleeding control or mortality versus a conventional centre. I found no international guideline that addresses hybrid rooms specifically for TCA.

### Cited Findings
- **RAPTOR, Calgary [2014]**: "the first such resource designed primarily to serve the exsanguinating trauma patient." It was funded philanthropically and purpose-built in a new hospital wing. The paper covers design, equipment, human-factors evaluation and a RAPTOR protocol. [Kirkpatrick et al., Injury 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24560091/)
- **Calgary cohort (2013–2019)**: truly simultaneous versus "rapid serial" open and percutaneous procedures had similar length of stay, ICU stay and mortality. (Search summary only) [Can J Surg 2022;65(5):E720](https://www.canjsurg.ca/content/65/5/E720)
- **RAPTOR suite, urban level I centre (2011–2016)**:
  - Only 1% of suite procedures were trauma-related: 43 trauma patients, mean ISS 38.
  - CT before treatment in 40%; DCS + angioembolization in 40%.
  - Median time to suite 56 min, total 251 min.
  - Earlier identification of patients who need the suite is advised.
  - The centre is not named in the abstract.
  - [Luijten et al., Eur J Trauma Emerg Surg 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36239761/)
- **UF Health dedicated trauma hybrid OR (USA)**:
  - n=186 vs 106 historical controls; OR arrival to haemorrhage control 49 vs 60 min.
  - Fewer 4–24 h RBC and plasma transfusions; fewer infections (15% vs 27%).
  - REBOA used in 9% vs 1%; mortality 13% vs 10% (NS).
  - [Loftus et al., J Am Coll Surg 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33227422/)
  - Value analysis: costs $50,023 vs $54,740 (NS); value unchanged. [Balch et al., J Trauma Acute Care Surg 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36727772/)
- **European military centres, Koblenz (hybrid, sliding CT) vs Toulon (conventional), 2013–2018**: n=607; time to CT 14 vs 30 min; time to bleeding control 92 vs 84 min (NS); 28-day mortality 17% in both. [de Malleray et al., Eur J Trauma Emerg Surg 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38289419/)
- **TEVAR for blunt aortic injury (PROOVIT, 14 US centres)**: portable C-arm OR cases had more complications and all 3 strokes. Fixed imaging (hybrid OR or IR suite) is safest. [Cabot et al., J Vasc Surg 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40464724/)
- **Implementation from the IR perspective (Toronto ACS level I centre) [2026]**: covers room zoning for laparotomy ↔ angioembolization transitions, a mobile supply cart, a pre-embolization checklist, and training in radiation safety, sterility and simulation. No outcome data. [Páez-Carpio et al., Cardiovasc Intervent Radiol 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42509488/)
- **US perspective on adopting Japanese HERS**: HERS "remains restricted to a small number of institutions." Obstacles in the US include building cost, return on investment, and differences in physician training, trauma-centre operation and reimbursement, which "may render the hybrid ER system to be unfeasible in most current trauma centers." [Tatum et al., Shock 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32205794/)
- **Low-resource alternative**: "Hybrid Trauma Service" model (Cali, Colombia, co-authored with Saiseikai Tobu): a trauma-surgeon-led vertical workflow that moves the patient rapidly between imaging, angiography and OR when a hybrid room is unaffordable. [Palacios-Rodríguez et al., Colomb Med 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8216051/)
- **Korea**: one Korean trauma centre had a HERS by 2019 (not named) [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/). The 2025 meta-analysis was by Korean authors, but all included studies were Japanese. [Kim 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41405346/)
- **RAPTOR general commentary**: high set-up and running costs, and "convincing outcome data showing benefit of RAPTOR suites is currently lacking." (Search summary only) [LITFL, RAPTOR suite](https://litfl.com/raptor-suite/)
- **TCA guidance**:
  - The only hybrid-room-specific guidance found is the JA-HERS 2021 statement: RT or REBOA should be preferred before CT in patients at risk of imminent CPA. [Wada 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/)
  - EAST [2025] REBOA guideline, per a search summary only: in TCA, pooled mortality favoured REBOA over RT (OR 0.32, 95% CI 0.15–0.69), with longer time to occlusion for REBOA. I did not read the guideline itself and the figures are unverified. [EAST 2025, PubMed 40166770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40166770/)
  - I found no ERC, ATLS or EAST statement about hybrid rooms or CT during CPR in TCA.

### Inferences
- The Japanese HERS is unusual internationally in putting CT, angiography and surgery inside the resuscitation bay as the default first destination. Western "hybrid ORs" are a downstream destination, and their reported benefit is in haemorrhage-control time and transfusion, not mortality.
- Koblenz shows that cutting time to CT alone does not automatically shorten time to haemostasis. This supports the Osaka authors' view that the integrated workflow and team, not the scanner, drive any benefit.

### Gaps
- No international data on TCA (arrest on arrival) managed in a hybrid room were found.
- European sliding-gantry dual-room CT trauma rooms (Kippnich; Frellesen) are referenced in Wada 2023 but were not retrieved in detail.

## 6. Practical limitations and risks: radiation, CPR during imaging (mechanical CPR), staffing, room occupancy, and when to stop

### Takeaway
The main documented downsides are:
- Patient radiation from trauma WBCT. Japanese trauma pan-scan doses are high (median 54 mSv), and staff dose in HERS has not been studied.
- Room occupancy by one patient for hours, which the dual-room design addresses.
- Possible infection risk from DCS in an ED room.
- High cost and space needs.
- A first-year learning curve with transiently worse outcomes.
- The need for 24/7 IR-capable staff.

CT during CPR has been done with mechanical compression devices (CLOVER3000) and scout-less, limited head and chest scans, but only in non-traumatic ECPR candidates. No Hybrid ER-specific termination rules for TCA were found.

### Cited Findings
- **Radiation, staff**: "there are no reports about assessment of radiation exposure in the hybrid ER"; dose should be reduced and assessed in future. [Wada 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/). Radiation exposure was a planned endpoint of the KAKEN multicentre project, but no results were reported. [KAKEN 20K10422](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/ja/grant/KAKENHI-PROJECT-20K10422)
- **Radiation, patient (not HERS-specific)**: Japanese nationwide questionnaire of trauma WBCT. Median effective dose 54.0 mSv, maximum 170.1 mSv; more than 100 mSv in 48 cases (8%). Doses are higher than abroad, so protocol standardization is called for. [Miyayasu et al., J Jpn Soc Emerg Med 2025;28(1):33-40](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jsem/28/1/28_33/_article/-char/en)
- **Image quality during resuscitation CT**: scanning with the backboard in place, arms not raised, and clothing, belts and monitoring metal still on, plus contrast in low-flow states, increases noise, streak and metal artefacts and gives poor enhancement. IVR-CT image-processing tools are used to mitigate this. [INNERVISION 2024](https://innervision.co.jp/ressources/pdf/innervision2024/iv202412_052.pdf)
- **CPR during CT**: continuous mechanical CPR (CLOVER3000) during head and chest CT without scout images. The compression device is visible on the CT images. [Ijuin 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9158146/); "During the scanning, mechanical CPR is continuously employed to avoid interruptions" (median arrival to end of CT 170 s). [Okada 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10801325/)
- **Scan time in near-arrest trauma**: median 5–6 minutes even without transfer. In the authors' words, "even CT scans that do not require patient transfer might be dangerous and not tolerated" in impending arrest. [Maruyama 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10825068/)
- **Room occupancy and throughput**: one severely injured patient occupies the room "for long periods," which reduces other diagnostic throughput. This motivated the dual-room design. During COVID-19, the KMU dual-room HERS did about 15,000 COVID CTs in 32 months while keeping trauma throughput. [Wada 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29506552/); [Wada 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/)
- **Infection**: "as the hybrid ER is located in the ER, there could be an increased risk of infectious complications with damage control surgery" (Ito et al., as cited). [Wada 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/). The Shimane design answers this with a class-10,000 air-conditioned OR. [Watanabe 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30223859/)
- **Cost and space**: about US$0.5–2 million plus space for machinery and control rooms. [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/). A suggested volume threshold to justify a hybrid room is about 150 trauma patients/year with ISS >16. [Palacios-Rodríguez 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8216051/)
- **Learning curve**:
  - Trauma mortality rose transiently in the first year at Osaka [JA-HERS 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6603312/), falling about 200 days after installation [Umemura 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33165282/).
  - ECPR times were prolonged just after hybrid ED installation at Tohoku. [Nakata 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11408869/)
- **Staffing**: 24/7 IR capability is needed, so train general and trauma surgeons in IR and hold regular simulation. [Wada 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231267/). Rapid callout of specialists such as vascular surgeons remains a bottleneck: "a delay remains until the vascular surgeons arrive." [Murai 2020 (Saiseikai Tobu)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7902278/). Low-frequency use and staffing are explicitly listed as practical challenges. [BeyondER 2024](https://webview.isho.jp/journal/detail/abs/10.11477/mf.3105200113)
- **Resource stewardship**: "use of the hybrid ER should be saved for critically ill patients"; it is "too resource intensive and time-sensitive for all emergencies." [Murai 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7902278/)
- **When to stop (non-trauma analogue)**: in the Hyogo PEA protocol, CT findings of ICH, pericardial effusion or massive haemothorax lead to withholding ECMO and continuing conventional CPR. CT is used as a decision tool to avoid futile ECPR. [Ijuin 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9158146/)

### Inferences
- For TCA, the Hybrid ER's main theoretical value is that RT or REBOA, DCS, TAE and ECMO can all start on one table, with CT brought in once there is perfusion. The main theoretical risk is "CT-ing a dying patient": 5–6 minutes of scanning plus image-degrading conditions, when aortic control or DCS should come first.
- Mechanical CPR devices may let scanning continue during arrest. In TCA, though, compressions have limited value in hypovolaemia, and devices add artefact and possible injury. This is inference; no TCA data were found.

### Gaps
- No staff-dose measurements in a HERS were found.
- No data on LUCAS or other mechanical CPR use during CT or angiography in TCA in a Hybrid ER.
- No published Hybrid ER-specific criteria for terminating resuscitation in TCA. Whether CT findings (for example, unsurvivable brain injury) are used to stop TCA resuscitation in Japanese HERS centres is unreported.
- I did not retrieve the JA-HERS 2021 guideline itself, which may contain termination or CT-tolerance criteria.
